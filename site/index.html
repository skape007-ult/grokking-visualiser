What am I looking at?

Grokking is when a neural network, long after it has perfectly memorised its training examples, suddenly starts getting examples it has never seen right. For a long stretch it looks like nothing is happening: training accuracy is already 100% and accuracy on new examples is near zero. Then, with no change to the setup, it "gets it", and accuracy on new examples shoots up to 100%.

What was done. The task is to answer (a + b) mod 97 for every pair of numbers from 0 to 96: 9,409 sums in all. A small transformer (one layer, about 220,000 parameters) was shown 30% of them (2,823 sums) and trained for 25,000 steps, then tested on the remaining 70% it had never seen. Training used the AdamW optimiser with weight decay 1.0, a penalty that constantly nudges the network's weights towards zero. The other settings each change exactly one thing from that setup.

What the toggle shows. In standard training the network hits 99% on its training examples by step 130, but only crosses 95% on unseen examples at step 10,065, 77× later. Remove weight decay and generalisation never comes: the network memorises just as fast, yet after 25,000 steps it is still right on only 3% of unseen examples, and its embeddings never form rings. With twice the data (60%), there is barely a delay: 95% on unseen examples by step 378. With half the data (15%), unseen accuracy stays at chance (1% at step 25,000). It may still grok later, but not within this training budget.

Papers: Power et al. (2022), Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets; Nanda et al. (2023), Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability. Code and data: REPO_URL.
